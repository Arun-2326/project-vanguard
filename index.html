Why it matters

Not shortcuts to the answer, but the feeling of discovering it.

3Blue1Brown videos rarely start with a definition. They start with a question, then let the animation do the arguing. Three habits make that work.

  1. 01 / question first

    Discovery over definition

    Lessons are framed so you could have invented the idea yourself — the formula arrives last, as a summary of something you already understand.

  2. 02 / visual proof

    Pictures that carry the argument

    Every animation is doing mathematical work. Grids shear, vectors spin and areas rescale because that is the explanation, not decoration on top of it.

  3. 03 / no condescension

    Respect for the viewer

    Hard topics stay hard, but honest. Pauses invite you to think, and the hardest step is never skipped with a hand-wave.

A short history

From a side project to a whole genre of math explainers.

The channel didn't just teach math. It open-sourced its tools and then spent its reach on helping other people make lessons of their own.

  1. 2015

    The channel begins

    Early videos on topics like the Hilbert curve and Euler's formula set the visual style.

  2. 2016

    Essence of Linear Algebra

    The series that made "a matrix is a transformation" click for countless students.

  3. 2017

    Calculus & neural networks

    Two more foundational series, including the now-classic intro to how networks learn.

  4. 2020

    Lockdown Math

    Live lessons for students learning from home, and a community fork of Manim takes off.

  5. 2021

    Summer of Math Exposition

    An open contest inviting anyone to make math explainers — and a flood of new creators.

  6. 2024

    Transformers, visually

    The neural network series reaches GPTs and attention, just when everyone needs it.

The tool behind the pictures

Every frame is code.

The animations are made with Manim, a Python library Grant wrote for his own videos and released as open source. A community edition now powers explainers, lectures and course material all over the world.

  • Scenes are written as programs, so every motion is mathematically exact.
  • LaTeX equations, graphs and 3D surfaces are first-class objects.
  • Anyone can install it and start making their own math videos.
